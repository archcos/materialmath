How to calculate baseboard

Baseboard runs around the edge of the room, so you need the perimeter, minus the width of each doorway. Add extra for miter cuts, then divide by the length of the pieces you are buying.

Baseboard = 2 × (length + width) − (doorways × doorway width)
Pieces = baseboard × (1 + extra %) ÷ piece length (round up)

Worked example

A 12 × 12 ft room with one 36 inch doorway, using 8 ft pieces:

  • Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 12) = 48 ft
  • Minus the doorway: 48 − 3 = 45 ft
  • With 10% extra: 49.5 ft
  • Pieces: 49.5 ÷ 8 = 6.2, so buy 7 pieces

Choosing piece lengths

Baseboard is commonly sold in 8, 12 and 16 ft lengths. Longer pieces cost more each but mean fewer joints along a wall, which looks cleaner. A good approach is to buy pieces long enough to run each wall in one go and use the offcuts on short walls and closets.

For the most accurate list, measure each wall and plan which piece goes where. The calculator's total is a good starting point for the budget.

Tips for a clean finish

  • Inside corners: coping (cutting the profile of one piece to fit over the other) gives a tighter joint than a plain miter, especially on walls that are not quite square.
  • Joints on long walls: join pieces with a scarf joint (two 45° cuts) over a stud so the seam is less visible.
  • Extra for mistakes: miter cuts are easy to get wrong. 10% extra is sensible, and 15% if this is your first time.
  • Matching shoe molding or quarter round needs about the same length again.

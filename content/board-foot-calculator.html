What is a board foot?

A board foot is a unit of lumber volume: a piece 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide and 12 inches long, or 144 cubic inches. Hardwood and rough lumber are usually priced per board foot, so knowing the count lets you compare prices and budget a project.

Board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12
or thickness (in) × width (in) × length (in) ÷ 144

Worked example

Ten boards, each 2 inches thick, 6 inches wide and 8 feet long:

  • One board: 2 × 6 × 8 ÷ 12 = 8 board feet
  • Ten boards: 8 × 10 = 80 board feet

At $6 per board foot, that would cost $480.

Nominal or actual size?

Board feet are normally calculated from the nominal (rough) dimensions, not the planed size. A construction 2×6 measures about 1½ × 5½ inches, but it is still counted as 2 × 6.

Hardwood thickness is often given in quarters of an inch:

Quarter sizeRough thickness
4/41 in
5/41¼ in
6/41½ in
8/42 in

So an 8/4 board is entered as 2 inches thick.

Metric equivalent

One board foot is about 0.00236 m³, so there are roughly 424 board feet in a cubic metre. Switch the calculator to metric to enter sizes in centimetres and metres.

Tips

  • Buy extra for defects. Knots, splits and uneven grain mean you will not use every inch. Woodworkers commonly add 20–30% for hardwood projects.
  • Random widths: rough hardwood often comes in random widths and lengths. Measure each board and add them up.

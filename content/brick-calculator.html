How to calculate bricks for a wall

Each brick, plus its share of the mortar joint, covers a fixed patch of wall. Divide the wall area by that patch, then multiply by the number of layers (wythes) if the wall is more than one brick thick.

Bricks = wall area ÷ area per brick (with joint) × layers

The figures assume a standard 3/8 inch (10 mm) mortar joint, laid in stretcher bond.

Worked example

A single-layer wall 20 feet long and 4 feet high in modular brick:

  • Wall area: 20 × 4 = 80 sq ft
  • Bricks: 80 × 6.75 = 540
  • Add 5% for breakage and cuts: 567 bricks

A double-layer wall, two bricks thick, needs twice as many: 1,134.

Tips for buying bricks

  • Subtract openings. Take the area of windows and doors off the wall area first.
  • Allow more waste for complex work. 5% suits a plain wall. Use 10% or more for many corners, piers or decorative patterns.
  • Buy from one batch. Brick color varies between production runs, so order everything at once and keep a few spares for future repairs.
  • Check the size. Reclaimed and specialty bricks often differ from the standard sizes. Measure one, add the joint, and work out the area per brick yourself.

How to calculate concrete blocks for a wall

Concrete blocks are sold by their nominal size, which already includes a 3/8 inch mortar joint. A standard US block is 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ inches, but with mortar it fills an 8 × 16 inch space on the wall face. Divide the wall area by the area of one block face.

Blocks = wall length × wall height ÷ area of one block face
8 × 16 in block: 128 sq in = 0.889 sq ft, so 1.125 blocks per sq ft

A metric 440 × 215 mm block with 10 mm joints fills 450 × 225 mm, which works out to almost exactly 10 blocks per square metre.

Worked example

A garden wall 20 feet long and 4 feet high:

  • Wall area: 20 × 4 = 80 sq ft
  • Blocks: 80 × 1.125 = 90 blocks
  • Add 5% for breakage and cuts: 94.5, so buy 95 blocks

The wall has 6 courses (4 ft = 48 in ÷ 8 in) of 15 blocks each (240 in ÷ 16 in).

Things the calculator does not include

  • Openings: for windows or doors, work out their area and subtract it from the wall area first.
  • Cap blocks: if you finish the top with caps, you need one per 16 inches of wall length (15 for the example wall).
  • Mortar: mortar coverage varies a lot by product and joint size, so check the bag. Mortar labels usually state roughly how many blocks one bag lays.
  • Footing: block walls need a solid concrete footing. Use the concrete slab calculator with the footing's length, width and depth.

Tips

  • Retaining walls and any wall taller than about 4 ft often need reinforcement and an engineer's design. Check your local code before you start.
  • Buy all blocks from one batch where possible so the color matches.

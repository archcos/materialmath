How to calculate concrete for a slab

A slab is a rectangular box, so the amount of concrete is simply its volume. Measure everything in the same unit first. In feet, that means turning the thickness from inches into feet by dividing by 12.

Volume (cu ft) = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (in) ÷ 12
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27

Ready-mix concrete is sold by the cubic yard (or cubic metre). Premix bags are sold by weight, and the bag label tells you how much volume it makes. A typical 80 lb bag yields about 0.6 cu ft, a 60 lb bag about 0.45 cu ft and a 40 lb bag about 0.3 cu ft.

Worked example

A 10 × 10 ft patio, 4 inches thick:

  • 10 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 33.3 cu ft
  • Add 10% for waste: 33.3 × 1.10 = 36.7 cu ft, or about 1.36 cu yd
  • 80 lb bags: 36.7 ÷ 0.6 = 61.1, so buy 62 bags

That is a lot of bags to mix by hand. Once a job needs more than about one cubic yard, a ready-mix delivery or a rented mixer usually saves time and often money.

How thick should a slab be?

UseTypical thickness
Walkways, patios, shed bases4 in (100 mm)
Driveways, hot tub pads5–6 in (125–150 mm)
Heavy vehicles, workshop floors6 in+ (150 mm+), usually with reinforcement

Tips for ordering the right amount

  • Measure depth in several places. Ground is rarely flat. Average your measurements, because an extra half inch across a slab adds up fast.
  • Never order exactly. Running short mid-pour leaves a weak cold joint. Spillage, form bulges and an uneven base all eat concrete, so keep 5–10% extra.
  • Check the bag. Yields vary slightly by brand and mix type, so confirm the yield printed on the bag you buy.

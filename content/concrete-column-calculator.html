How to calculate concrete for a post hole

A round hole or column form is a cylinder. Its volume is the area of the circle times the depth. When a post sits in the hole, the post takes up space, so subtract it.

Hole volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth
Concrete = hole volume − (post width × post width × depth)

For a solid column or a tube form with no post, enter 0 for the post width.

Worked example

One fence post hole, 10 inches across and 3 feet deep, with a 4×4 post (which actually measures 3.5 × 3.5 inches):

  • Hole area: 3.1416 × 5² = 78.5 sq in
  • Post area: 3.5 × 3.5 = 12.25 sq in
  • Concrete area: 78.5 − 12.25 = 66.3 sq in
  • Volume: 66.3 × 36 in = 2,386 cu in = 1.38 cu ft
  • Add 10%: 1.52 cu ft, or about 2.5 bags of 80 lb mix, so buy 3 per hole

How big should the hole be?

  • Width: a common rule is a hole about three times the width of the post. That means 10–12 inches for a 4×4 and 14–18 inches for a 6×6.
  • Depth: about one third to one half of the post's height above ground, and below your local frost line in cold climates. Your local building department can tell you the frost depth.
  • Drainage: many builders add a few inches of gravel at the bottom so water drains away from the post end.

Tips

  • Fast-setting post mix can be poured dry into the hole and then watered, which saves mixing. Check the bag for its yield, since some fast-set mixes yield less per bag.
  • Holes dug by hand are rarely perfect cylinders and usually flare at the top. The 10% extra covers this, but add more for loose or sandy soil.
  • For tube forms (Sonotubes), use the tube diameter and the full height of the form.

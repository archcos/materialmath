How the cut list is worked out

The calculator sorts your pieces from longest to shortest and places each one on the first board that still has room. A new board is started only when no board has space left. This "longest first" method is how carpenters lay out cuts by hand, and it wastes very little wood.

Room on a board = board length − end trim
Each piece uses its length + one saw kerf (the last piece may end right at the board's end)
Boards to buy = boards the cuts fit on × (1 + extra), rounded up

Every piece is cut from a board of the same width and thickness, so make one list per board size: one for your 1×4s, another for your 2×4s.

Worked example: a simple bookshelf

From 8 ft (96 in) boards, with a ⅛ in blade and ½ in trimmed off one factory end of each board: 2 sides at 46 in, 4 shelves at 30 in, a top at 32 in and a toe kick at 28½ in.

  • Board 1: 46 + 46 in, leaving a 3¼ in offcut.
  • Board 2: 32 + 30 + 30 in, leaving 3⅛ in.
  • Board 3: 30 + 30 + 28½ in, leaving 6⅝ in.
  • The 8 cuts turn 1 in of wood into sawdust. The cuts fit on 3 boards; with 10% extra for defects, buy 4.

Why not just add up the lengths? Make the sides 47¾ in instead. The total is 276 in, which divided by 96 still looks like under 3 boards. But two 47¾ in pieces plus a kerf need 95⅝ in, more than the 95½ in left after trimming, so the sides can no longer share a board and the cuts need 4 boards. Adding up lengths ignores where the cuts fall.

Measuring and marking for accurate cuts

  • Measure twice, cut once. Use the same tape for the whole project: two tapes can differ by 1/16 in.
  • Mark with a V or a knife line, not a thick pencil dash. The point of the V is the exact length.
  • Cut on the waste side of the line. Keep the line on the piece you are keeping, so the kerf comes out of the offcut.
  • Square the line across with a speed square or combination square before you cut.
  • Matching pieces: cut the first one, then use it (or a stop block on the saw) to mark the rest, so they are exactly the same.
  • Mark each piece as you cut it ("left side", "shelf 2") so it does not get mixed up.

Buying lumber

  • Boards are sold in 2 ft steps: 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 and 16 ft (1.8 m to 4.8 m). Try the next length up: sometimes one 10 ft board saves a whole extra 8 ft one.
  • A "1×4" is really ¾ × 3½ in. The joint calculator has the full table of real sizes.
  • Sight down each board for bow, twist and cup, and skip ones with big knots near where you will cut joints.
  • Let lumber sit in the room where you will build for a few days, so it can adjust before you cut it.
  • Hardwood priced by volume? Use the Board Foot Calculator.

Plywood and sheet goods

This calculator plans lengths. For plywood and MDF, sketch the 4 × 8 ft (1.22 × 2.44 m) sheet on paper and fit the parts on it, allowing a kerf between each. Rip the sheet into strips first, then crosscut the strips. Most stores will make the first rip cuts for you.

Safety

  • Support both sides of a long board so the offcut does not drop and pinch the blade.
  • Never hold short pieces close to the blade by hand. Use a clamp, a push stick or a stop block.
  • Wear eye and hearing protection, and let the blade stop before you reach for the offcut.

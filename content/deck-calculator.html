How to calculate deck boards

This calculator assumes the boards run along the deck's length and the joists run across it. First work out how many rows of boards fit across the width, including the gap between boards. Then multiply by the length to get the total length of board, and divide by the length of one board.

Rows = deck width ÷ (board width + gap) (round up)
Boards = rows × deck length × (1 + extra %) ÷ board length
Joists = deck length ÷ joist spacing + 1

Use the actual board width, not the nominal size. A "6 inch" (5/4 × 6 or 2 × 6) deck board is really about 5.5 inches wide.

Worked example

A 16 × 12 ft deck with 5.5 inch boards, ⅛ inch gaps, 16 ft boards and joists every 16 inches:

  • Rows: 144 in ÷ (5.5 + 0.125) = 25.6, so 26 rows
  • Board length: 26 × 16 ft = 416 ft
  • With 10% extra: 458 ft ÷ 16 = 28.6, so buy 29 boards
  • Joists: 192 in ÷ 16 = 12, plus one, so 13 joists (each 12 ft long)

Joist spacing

  • 16 inches on center is standard for most wood decking.
  • 12 inches on center is often required for composite decking, and for boards laid diagonally.
  • Always follow the decking manufacturer's maximum span, and your local code's span tables for the joists themselves.

Tips

  • Match board length to deck length where possible. A 16 ft deck with 16 ft boards has no butt joints in the field.
  • Gaps: wet pressure-treated wood shrinks as it dries, so it is often laid tight. Composite usually needs the gap the manufacturer specifies.
  • The frame needs more: rim joists, beams, posts, footings, blocking, fasteners and hangers are extra. Use the post hole calculator for the footings.

How to calculate drywall sheets

Add up the area of the walls (and the ceiling, if you are covering it), add extra for cuts, then divide by the area of one sheet.

Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
Sheets = (wall area + ceiling area) × (1 + extra %) ÷ sheet area

Worked example

A 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, walls only, using 4 × 8 sheets:

  • Wall area: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 sq ft
  • With 10% extra: 422 sq ft
  • Sheets: 422 ÷ 32 = 13.2, so buy 14 sheets

Covering the ceiling as well adds 144 sq ft, for 19 sheets in total.

Why the calculator does not subtract doors and windows

Most installers hang full sheets over small openings and cut them out afterwards, and the offcuts are rarely big enough to use elsewhere. Only subtract very large openings such as patio doors or wide archways.

Screws, tape and mud

  • Screws: plan on about one screw per square foot, roughly 32 for a 4 × 8 sheet on walls.
  • Joint tape and compound: check the coverage on the products you buy. Ready-mixed compound labels usually state the approximate area a bucket finishes.

Tips

  • Longer sheets mean fewer joints. If you can get 12 ft sheets into the room, you will have less taping to do.
  • Thickness: ½ inch is standard for walls. Thicker or fire-rated board is common on ceilings, garages and shared walls, so check your local code.
  • Bathrooms: use moisture-resistant board in damp areas and cement board behind tile in showers.

How to calculate fence posts and panels

A straight fence is a row of sections (or panels) with a post at each end of every section. Divide the fence length by the post spacing and round up to get the number of sections. A straight run always needs one more post than sections.

Sections = fence length ÷ post spacing (round up)
Posts = sections + 1
Rails = sections × rails per section

Worked example

A 100 foot fence with posts every 8 feet and 2 rails per section:

  • Sections: 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so 13 sections
  • Posts: 13 + 1 = 14 posts
  • Rails: 13 × 2 = 26 rails

Because 100 is not a multiple of 8, one section will be shorter. You can also respace the posts evenly: 100 ÷ 13 = about 7 ft 8 in apart.

Corners and gates

The calculator handles one straight run. For a fence with corners, calculate each side separately. Then subtract one post for each corner, since neighbouring sides share the corner post. Gates need their own sturdy posts on both sides, and usually a heavier post size.

Common post spacing

  • Wood privacy and picket fences: 6–8 ft. Most pre-built panels are 8 ft wide (or 6 ft).
  • Vinyl fences: match the panel width exactly, usually 6 or 8 ft.
  • Wire and farm fencing: 8–12 ft, with braced posts at corners and ends.

Tips

  • Check your property line and local rules before you dig. Many areas limit fence height and require permits.
  • Call before you dig so underground utilities are marked.

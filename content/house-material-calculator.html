How the house calculator works

A building needs many materials at once, so this calculator runs the site's other calculators together from a few measurements and adds the results up into one list and cost total. Pick a sample build to start, then change the numbers to match your plans.

It assumes a rectangular building on a concrete slab, with the roof ridge running along the length. Each part uses the same math as its own calculator, so you can open that calculator to check or fine-tune any line.

Make it your own

OptionWhat it changes
StoriesTwo stories doubles the outside walls, siding and wall insulation, and adds upper-floor joists and subfloor. The slab, roof and attic stay the same size.
Upper floor wall heightThe upper floor can be taller or lower than the ground floor. Its siding, drywall, paint and insulation follow its own height.
Roof styleA gable roof has two slopes and triangular end walls. A hip roof slopes on all four sides: no gable siding, but four hips that take ridge cap.
Roof overhangHow far the roof sticks out past the walls, which changes the roof area, shingles and drip edge.
Walls2×4 walls get R-13 batts; thicker 2×6 walls get R-21. Concrete block (CMU or hollow block) swaps the studs, plates, wall sheathing and batts for blocks and mortar, with render on the inside walls and drywall on the ceilings only.
Stud spacing24 in (600 mm) uses about a quarter fewer studs than 16 in (400 mm). Not used for block walls.
RoofingAsphalt shingles (bundles and ridge cap), metal panels (panels cut to the eave-to-ridge length, and metal ridge cap) or concrete or clay tiles (about 90 per square, or 10.5 per m², and ridge tiles).
Outside finishVinyl or fiber cement siding by area, stucco or cement render in 80 lb (25 kg) bags (about 11 sq ft, or 0.65 m², per bag at ¾ in or 20 mm), or brick veneer with its bricks and mortar.
FlooringLaminate or vinyl plank, tile, hardwood or carpet, each priced on its own. Tile adds bags of tile adhesive (thinset).
Garage doorsEach 16 × 7 ft door comes out of the siding and sheathing.

Sample layout and 3D view

Next to the calculator, the 3D view shows the building from your numbers: walls, doors, windows, the roof and, inside, a sample room layout. Drag it to turn it, scroll or pinch to zoom, or press Hide roof to look inside. On a two-story house, Hide upper floor lets you see into the ground floor. The Plan tab draws each floor like a blueprint, with the rooms, door swings and windows, and the overall size.

The sample layout is only an illustration of where the inside walls you entered might go. It puts the bedrooms and bath along the back and the living space at the front.

Draw your own floor plan

To plan your own house, open the Plan tab and press Draw my own layout. You start from the sample and change it with the tools above the drawing:

ToolWhat it does
WallDrag to draw an inside wall. It runs straight across or up and down, and snaps to a 6 in (10 cm) grid and to the walls already there. The length shows as you drag.
DoorClick a wall to add a door. On an outside wall it is an outside door (3 ft); on an inside wall, an inside door (32 in).
WindowClick an outside wall to add a window (3 ft wide).
Garage doorClick a ground-floor outside wall to add a 16 × 7 ft garage door.
Room nameClick inside a room to label it, or click a label to rename it.
EraseClick a wall, door, window or label to remove it. The outside walls stay: change the building size to move them.

For a two-story house, switch between Ground floor and Upper floor to draw each one. The upper floor shows the ground floor's walls faintly underneath, and new walls snap to them so they line up. Undo (or Ctrl+Z) steps back, and Back to the sample drops your drawing.

While you draw, the estimate follows your plan. It measures your inside walls and counts your doors and windows floor by floor, so the "Inside walls", "Outside doors", "Garage doors", "Inside doors" and "Windows" fields fill in by themselves, and the framing, drywall, baseboard, paint and siding update. The 3D view shows your layout too. Your drawing is saved in this browser, so it is still there when you come back.

Nails, screws and small items

With "Nails, screws and small items" set to include them (the default), the list ends with the fasteners and small items for your build. Nails are counted in 5 lb boxes in imperial units and by the kilo in metric, the way stores sell them. Choose "Leave them out" for the big materials only; they usually add about 5% to the total.

What is not included

  • Windows and doors, and the headers above them
  • Plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling
  • Kitchen and bathroom cabinets, counters and fixtures
  • Flashing, tools, scaffolding and anything the build does not show, like a porch or stairs
  • Permits, delivery and labor

Those usually cost much more than the materials listed here, so treat the total as one part of the budget.

Tips

  • Two stories: enter the inside walls, inside doors and windows for the whole house; the calculator splits them between the floors. Add the staircase with the stair calculator.
  • L-shaped or bigger plans: split the building into rectangles and run each one. Press + Add to project after each, and My project adds them into one list.
  • Check with your supplier. Once you have plans, a lumberyard can do a full takeoff. Use this estimate to budget and to sanity-check their numbers.
  • Garages: enter garage doors separately from ordinary doors. Each one takes a 16 × 7 ft opening out of the siding and sheathing.

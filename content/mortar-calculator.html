How to calculate mortar

First count the bricks or blocks in the wall: the wall area divided by the face area of one unit, including its joint. Then multiply by how much mortar each unit takes.

Units = wall area ÷ area per unit (with joint) × layers
Bags of mortar mix = units ÷ units laid per bag

The US figures come from Quikrete's data sheet for its 80 lb Mortar Mix. UK bagged mortar makers quote 20–25 bricks and about 12 blocks per 25 kg bag, with 10 mm joints. Your brand may differ a little, so check the bag.

Mixing your own

For a big wall, buying cement and sand separately costs less than premixed bags. The calculator splits the same total weight into:

  • US: masonry cement (70 lb bags) and mason sand, mixed 1 part cement to 3 parts sand by volume. That is about 19% cement by weight.
  • UK: cement (25 kg bags) and building sand, mixed about 1 to 5 with a plasticiser. That is about 15% cement by weight. 1,000 bricks need about 8 bags of cement and a tonne of sand.

Worked example

A single-layer wall 20 ft long and 4 ft high in US modular brick, with 5% extra bricks:

  • Bricks: 80 sq ft × 6.75 = 540, plus 5% = 567 bricks
  • Mortar mix: 567 ÷ 37 = 15.3, so buy 16 bags of 80 lb
  • Or mix your own: about 1,230 lb in total, which is 4 bags of masonry cement plus about half a ton of mason sand

Tips

  • Buy one extra bag. Running out halfway through a course leaves a visible line where the batches meet.
  • Mix in small batches. Use mortar within about 2 hours, so mix only what you can lay in that time.
  • Count both layers. A double-layer wall uses twice the bricks and twice the mortar. Add 10–20% more if you fill the gap between layers.

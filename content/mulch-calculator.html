How to calculate mulch

Mulch is spread as an even layer, so the amount you need is area × depth. Bulk mulch is sold by the cubic yard, and bagged mulch usually in 2 cubic foot bags (or 50 to 70 litre bags in metric countries).

Volume (cu ft) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27
2 cu ft bags = cubic feet ÷ 2

A handy rule: one cubic yard of mulch covers about 108 sq ft at 3 inches deep, or about 162 sq ft at 2 inches.

Worked example

A 10 × 10 foot bed with 3 inches of mulch:

  • 10 × 10 × (3 ÷ 12) = 25 cu ft
  • Add 5%: 26.25 cu ft = 0.97 cu yd
  • Bags: 26.25 ÷ 2 = 13.1, so buy 14 bags of 2 cu ft

Around 14 bags is roughly where bulk delivery starts to compete on price, depending on local delivery fees.

How deep should mulch be?

  • 2–3 inches is the usual target for flower and shrub beds. It holds moisture and blocks most weeds.
  • Up to 4 inches for coarse wood chips on paths or under play equipment.
  • Top-ups: mulch breaks down, so most beds need 1 inch added each year rather than a full new layer.

Tips

  • Keep mulch off tree trunks and plant stems. Piling mulch against bark (a "mulch volcano") traps moisture and invites rot and pests. Leave a few inches of gap.
  • Bag sizes vary. Some bags hold 1.5 or 3 cu ft. Check the bag and divide your cubic feet by that number instead.
  • For irregular beds, split them into rectangles, or measure the longest length and average width.

How to calculate paint

Choose what you are painting: a room, a house exterior, or an area you already know. For a room or house, the calculator finds the total wall area, take away the doors and windows you will not paint, multiply by the number of coats, then divide by the paint's coverage.

Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
Paintable area = wall area − doors − windows (+ ceiling if painting it)
Gallons = paintable area × coats ÷ 350

This calculator allows about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window. Coverage depends on the surface:

350 sq ft is the low end of the 350–400 sq ft most interior paint labels state, so you are unlikely to run short.

Worked example

A 12 × 12 ft bedroom with 8 ft walls, one door and two windows, painted with two coats:

  • Walls: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 sq ft
  • Minus door and windows: 384 − 20 − 30 = 334 sq ft
  • Two coats: 668 sq ft
  • Paint: 668 ÷ 350 = 1.9 gallons, so buy 2 gallons

Paint for a house exterior

For the outside of a house, enter the length and width of the house and the wall height from the foundation to the roof line. Gable ends (the triangles under a pitched roof) are extra: add about width × gable height ÷ 2 for each one, or use the "I know the area" option.

Painting by square footage

If you already know the wall area, choose "I know the area" and type it in. Don't use the floor area of the house: walls usually add up to several times the floor area. At 350 sq ft per gallon, 1,500 sq ft of wall needs about 9 gallons for two coats.

When you need more paint

  • Big color changes: going from dark to light (or the reverse) may need a primer coat plus two top coats.
  • Textured or porous surfaces: new drywall, bare plaster and textured walls soak up more paint. Prime new drywall first.
  • Rough or exterior surfaces: brick, stucco and rough wood can need up to twice as much.

Tips

  • Buy it all at once. Tinted paint can vary slightly between batches. If you need several cans, mix them together in a bucket ("boxing") for an even color.
  • Trim and doors: trim paint is usually bought separately, often a quart is enough for a room's trim.
  • Keep leftovers labelled with the room name for touch-ups.

How to calculate pavers

Divide the area of your patio or path by the face area of one paver, then add extra for cuts at the edges. Paver joints are only a few millimetres of sand, so they barely affect the count.

Pavers = (area length × area width) ÷ (paver length × paver width)
Use the same units throughout, for example inches² for both

Worked example

A 10 × 10 foot patio using 4 × 8 inch pavers:

  • Patio area: 100 sq ft = 14,400 sq in
  • Paver area: 4 × 8 = 32 sq in, so 4.5 pavers per sq ft
  • Pavers: 14,400 ÷ 32 = 450
  • Add 5% for cuts: 473 pavers

How much extra to add

LayoutExtra
Straight rectangle, running bond5%
Herringbone or 45° patterns10–15%
Curves and circles15% or more

Don't forget the base

Pavers need a solid base to stay level. A typical patio build-up is:

  • 4–6 inches of compacted crushed gravel for patios and walkways (more for driveways). Work it out with the gravel calculator.
  • About 1 inch of bedding sand on top, screeded flat.
  • Edge restraints around the outside to stop the pavers spreading.
  • Joint sand (often polymeric sand) swept into the gaps when finished.

Tips

  • Many pavers are sold by the square foot or by the pallet. Divide your total area by the coverage on the pallet label to compare prices.
  • Keep a few spare pavers from the same batch for future repairs. Colors change between production runs.

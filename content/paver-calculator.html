How to calculate pavers

Divide the area of your patio or path by the face area of one paver, then add extra for cuts at the edges. Most pavers have spacer nubs, so the joint is already part of their size. If you lay pavers with a wider gap, enter it and the calculator adds it to each paver.

Pavers = area ÷ ((paver length + gap) × (paver width + gap))
Use the same units throughout, for example inches² for both

Worked example

A 10 × 10 foot patio using 4 × 8 inch pavers:

  • Patio area: 100 sq ft = 14,400 sq in
  • Paver area: 4 × 8 = 32 sq in, so 4.5 pavers per sq ft
  • Pavers: 14,400 ÷ 32 = 450
  • Add 5% for cuts: 473 pavers
  • Gravel base: 100 sq ft × 4 in = 33.3 cu ft, plus 20% = 40 cu ft, or 1.5 cubic yards (about 2.1 tons)
  • Bedding sand: 100 sq ft × 1 in = 8.3 cu ft, plus 10% = 9.2 cu ft, or 19 bags of 0.5 cu ft

The base under the pavers

Pavers need a solid base to stay level. The calculator works out the gravel and sand for you, with 20% extra gravel and 10% extra sand because both compact. A typical patio build-up is:

  • 4–6 inches of compacted crushed gravel for patios and walkways (more for driveways).
  • About 1 inch of bedding sand on top, screeded flat. Bagged sand is usually 0.5 cu ft (about 50 lb).
  • Edge restraints around the outside to stop the pavers spreading.
  • Joint sand (often polymeric sand) swept into the gaps when finished.

Tips

  • Many pavers are sold by the square foot or by the pallet. Divide your total area by the coverage on the pallet label to compare prices.
  • Keep a few spare pavers from the same batch for future repairs. Colors change between production runs.

How to calculate rebar for a slab

Rebar in a slab is laid as a grid, with bars running both ways at the same spacing. Keep the bars a few inches in from the edges so concrete covers them. Then count the bars in each direction and add up their lengths.

Bars lengthwise = (width − 2 × edge) ÷ spacing + 1
Bars widthwise = (length − 2 × edge) ÷ spacing + 1
Total length = bars × their length + lap splices

Where a run is longer than one bar, two bars overlap. The calculator uses a lap of 40 bar diameters, a common rule: 15 in for #3, 20 in for #4 and 25 in for #5.

Worked example

A 20 × 20 ft slab with #4 bar at 12 in spacing, 3 in from the edges, using 20 ft bars:

  • Each run is 20 ft − 6 in = 19.5 ft, so no splices are needed
  • Bars each way: 19.5 ÷ 1 + 1 = 20
  • Total length: 40 × 19.5 = 780 ft, about 521 lb
  • Bars to buy: 780 × 1.05 ÷ 20 = 41 bars
  • Tie points: 20 × 20 = 400 crossings to tie with wire

Tips

  • Lift the grid. Set the rebar on chairs or small concrete blocks so it ends up near the middle of the slab. Bars lying in the dirt do little.
  • Tie every other crossing. Many crews tie every second intersection in a slab, and every one at the edges.
  • Follow your plans. Footings, structural slabs and anything under a building need the bar size and spacing your plans or engineer call for.

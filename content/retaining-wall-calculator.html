How to calculate retaining wall blocks

A segmental retaining wall is built from rows (courses) of interlocking blocks stacked without mortar. Work out how many courses you need to reach the height, add one course buried below ground, then multiply by the blocks in each course.

Courses = wall height ÷ block height (rounded up) + 1 buried
Blocks = courses × wall length ÷ block length
Cap blocks = wall length ÷ cap length

Gravel for the base and drainage

The calculator also works out the gravel, using common installation guidance:

  • Base: a trench 6 in (15 cm) deep and 12 in (30 cm) wider than the block depth, filled with compacted crushed stone.
  • Drainage: 12 in (30 cm) of clean gravel behind the wall, the full height of the blocks, with a perforated drain pipe at the bottom.
  • Both get 20% extra, because gravel compacts when you tamp it down.

Worked example

A wall 20 ft long and 2 ft high, using 12 × 4 in blocks that are 7 in deep, with 12 in caps:

  • Courses: 24 in ÷ 4 in = 6, plus 1 buried = 7
  • Blocks: 7 × 20 = 140, plus 5% = 147 blocks
  • Caps: 20 + 5% = 21 cap blocks
  • Gravel: about 19 cu ft for the base and 56 cu ft for drainage, which is 2.8 cubic yards (about 4 tons)
  • Drain pipe: 20 ft

Tips

  • Get the first course level. It sets the line for the whole wall. Spend the most time here.
  • Step the base on a slope. Where the ground falls along the wall, step the trench down in block-height steps.
  • Know when to get help. Walls over about 3–4 ft, or walls holding up a driveway, slope or building, usually need geogrid, an engineered design and a permit.

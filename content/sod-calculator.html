How to calculate sod

Sod is sold by area, so you need the area of your lawn plus a little extra for trimming around edges, beds and paths.

Sod = lawn length × lawn width × (1 + extra %)
Pieces = sod area ÷ area of one piece

In the US, sod usually comes in pieces (slabs) of about 2 × 5 ft (10 sq ft), stacked on pallets of roughly 400 to 500 sq ft. This calculator uses 450 sq ft per pallet. In metric countries, turf is usually sold in rolls of 1 m².

Worked example

A lawn 20 feet by 25 feet:

  • Area: 20 × 25 = 500 sq ft
  • Add 5% for cuts: 525 sq ft
  • Pieces: 525 ÷ 10 = 52.5, so buy 53 pieces
  • Pallets: 525 ÷ 450 = 1.2, so 2 pallets (or 1 pallet plus loose pieces)

Measuring an irregular lawn

Split the lawn into rectangles, triangles and circles, work out each one and add them together:

  • Rectangle: length × width
  • Triangle: base × height ÷ 2
  • Circle: 3.14 × radius × radius

For curvy edges, add 10% instead of 5%.

Tips for laying sod

  • Lay it the day it arrives. Sod dries out and heats up quickly on the pallet, so plan delivery for when you are ready.
  • Prepare the soil first. Loosen the ground and add 4–6 inches of good soil if needed (see the topsoil calculator).
  • Stagger the seams like brickwork and butt the edges tight without overlapping.
  • Water straight away and keep it moist until the roots take hold, usually two to three weeks.

How to calculate stairs

Start with the total rise: the vertical distance from the finished floor at the bottom to the finished floor at the top. Divide it by the tallest riser you allow and round up. That gives the number of risers, which you then divide back into the rise so every step is the same height.

Risers = total rise ÷ maximum riser height (round up)
Riser height = total rise ÷ risers
Treads = risers − 1
Total run = treads × tread depth
Stringer length = √(rise² + run²)

There is one fewer tread than risers because the top floor itself acts as the last step.

Worked example

A total rise of 108 inches (9 ft), a 7¾ inch maximum riser and 10 inch treads:

  • Risers: 108 ÷ 7.75 = 13.9, so 14 risers
  • Riser height: 108 ÷ 14 = 7.71 inches
  • Treads: 13
  • Total run: 13 × 10 = 130 inches (10 ft 10 in)
  • Stringer: √(108² + 130²) = 169 inches, about 14.1 ft, so a 16 ft board is needed for each stringer

Building code limits

In the US, the International Residential Code (IRC) used by most areas sets a maximum riser of 7¾ inches and a minimum tread depth of 10 inches for homes. Riser heights in one flight should not vary by more than ⅜ inch. Other countries and some local codes differ, so always check yours, along with the rules for headroom, handrails and guards.

Making stairs comfortable

  • A common comfort rule is riser + tread ≈ 17 to 18 inches. The example gives 7.71 + 10 = 17.7.
  • Shorter risers and deeper treads feel easier but take up more floor space.

Tips

  • Measure finished floor to finished floor. If flooring is still to be installed, allow for its thickness at the top and bottom, or the first and last steps will be uneven.
  • Stringer boards are usually 2×12 lumber. Buy them longer than the calculated stringer length to allow for the cuts at each end.

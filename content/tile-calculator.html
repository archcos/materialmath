How to calculate tiles

Divide the area you are tiling by the area of one tile, then add extra for cuts and breakage. Grout joints are thin enough that they barely change the count, so the calculator leaves them out, which errs on the safe side.

Tiles = area × (1 + extra %) ÷ (tile length × tile width)
Boxes = tiles ÷ tiles per box (round up)

Worked example

A 10 × 10 ft floor with 12 × 12 inch tiles sold 10 to a box:

  • Area: 100 sq ft
  • One tile: 1 sq ft
  • With 10% extra: 110 tiles
  • Boxes: 110 ÷ 10 = 11 boxes

How much extra to add

LayoutExtra
Straight grid in a simple room10%
Diagonal or herringbone15%
Large-format tiles (24 in+) or many obstacles15–20%

Large tiles waste more because each cut offcut is bigger and less likely to be reused.

Tips

  • Check the box coverage. Tile boxes list the square feet they cover. If you know it, divide your area (with extra) by that figure for a quick cross-check.
  • Keep a spare box. Tiles are often discontinued, and a few spares make future repairs invisible.
  • Check the lot number. Make sure every box has the same shade or lot number, since color varies between batches.
  • Walls: the same math works for a backsplash or shower wall. Use the wall width and height instead of length and width.
